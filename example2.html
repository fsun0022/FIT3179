Act I: Geography & Infrastructure

1. Mapping the Outback’s Living Inventory

Across Australia’s vast landscape, cattle outnumber humans in key regions. Over 2 million head roam an island continent characterized by extreme climate divides—from northern tropical savannas where extensive herds graze across vast properties, to intensive southern feedlot systems feeding Australia’s grain-fed premium sector.

Key Insight: Queensland alone holds over 40% of the national herd, acting as the primary engine for grass-fed breeding and finishing operations.

Act III: International Trade

3. Feeding a Hungry World

Australia exports more than 70% of the beef it produces, acting as a critical food supplier for North America and Asia. When drought squeezes international competitors, Australian red meat fills global supply gaps—navigating complex trade agreements, live export dynamics, and shifting consumer preferences.

Act IV: Climate & Future Horizon

4. Weathering the Storm: Rebuilding vs. Liquidation

The ultimate fate of the Australian beef industry is governed by rainfall. When grass is abundant, producers retain breeding females to rebuild herds. When severe dry spells strike, high Female Slaughter Rates (FSR) signal herd liquidation. Balancing domestic appetite with climate resilience remains the industry's ultimate challenge.
